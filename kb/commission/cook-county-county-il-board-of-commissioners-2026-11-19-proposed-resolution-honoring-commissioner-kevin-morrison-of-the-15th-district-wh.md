Agenda Item: PROPOSED RESOLUTION HONORING COMMISSIONER KEVIN MORRISON OF THE 15TH DISTRICT WHEREAS, Commissioner Kevin Morrison was first elected in 2018 to represent the 15th district and became the first Democrat to hold the seat, ousting incumbent third-term Commissioner and then Illinois GOP Chairman Tim Schneider; and WHEREAS, with Commissioner Morrison’s election, he became the first openly LGBTQ+ and Pansexual Commissioner in Cook County history, as well as the youngest Commissioner at the time to serve on the Cook County and Forest Preserve District Boards; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison is a life-long Cook County resident, born in Chicago, and raised in Elk Grove Village; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison is the grandson of Italian immigrants who through hard work and dedication, achieved the American Dream; and WHEREAS, the legacy created by Umberto Borrini and Concetta (Tina) Borrini lives on today 58 years later through their daughters and now current owners Bianca Henniges and Bruna Morrison, who run the restaurant they created, keeping the legacy alive for generations to come; and WHEREAS, this small business, Dino’s Pizza and Italian Restaurant, and his grandparents’ immigration story inspired him to run for the Cook County Board in 2018, to bring the leadership that was needed in making Cook County a better place for our current small businesses, and to improve the environment making it easier for aspiring small business owners to start new ones; and WHEREAS, his mother Bruna Morrison, stepfather Darek Wiktorowicz, siblings Christina Morrison Ducat and Matthew Wiktorowicz, and fiancé Ethan Ogborn, as well as his nephews Marco and Niccolo, and niece Liviana, have all inspired him to be a better leader, son, brother, partner, and uncle; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison graduated from James B. Conant High School in Hoffman Estates, Illinois in 2009; and WHEREAS, in 2013, Commissioner Morrison earned his bachelor's degree in Political Science, Environmental Science, and LGBTQ+ Studies from DePaul University in Chicago; and WHEREAS, after five and a half years of taking night classes, Commissioner Morrison received his Juris Doctorate, graduating Cum Laude, from the University of Illinois Chicago School of Law in 2024, and after passing the Illinois Bar Exam on his first attempt, became a licensed attorney in May of 2025; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison has been a life-long activist and advocate, focusing on organizing with communities for better representation, environmental causes, public service, and advocacy; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison began working at the age of sixteen, first working at his neighborhood Walgreens, where he worked throughout high school; and WHEREAS, throughout college until 2015, Commissioner Morrison was proud to work at his family’s restaurant, interned for Alderpersons Mary O’Connor and James Cappleman, as well as volunteer and work positions for a number of environmental causes, state legislature races, and local campaigns; and WHEREAS, his passion for organizing continued when he had the incredible opportunity to work for Hillary Clinton’s Presidential Campaign, where he worked as a Regional Organizing Director and Organizer from 2015 through 2016 for Hillary for America, stationed in Northeast Ohio during the general election, and during the primaries and caucuses in Maricopa County, Arizona, Jefferson County, Colorado, and Tama County, Iowa; and WHEREAS, this dedication to advocacy and public service was followed by his work in 2017 as the Lead Canvassing Director for Zero Week Solutions in Los Angeles, California, which at the time was the most expensive school board race in the nation, where he worked on behalf of the public schools’ coalition that was being drastically outspent by a Charter Schools PAC; and WHEREAS, after that campaign concluded, Commissioner Morrison returned home, and was honored to accept a position working for his Congressman, serving as the Special Projects Coordinator for Congressman Raja Krishnamoorthi; and WHEREAS, always interested in finding ways to better serve his community, Commissioner Morrison took the bold step to launch a campaign for the Cook County Board at just the age of 27 years old; and WHEREAS, after being sworn in as Cook County Commissioner in December 2018, Commissioner Morrison had the honor to become a Board member of the LGBTQ+ Victory Fund, the only organization that supports openly LGBTQ+ candidates up and down the ballot, and across the nation, where he served from 2019 through 2024; and WHEREAS, during his time on the Victory Fund Board, Commissioner Morrison personally raised $35,000 annually for the organization, guided organizational strategy and governance decisions, as well as supporting the advancement of LGBTQ+ leadership by providing resources, support and programming to elected LGBTQ+ elected and appointed officials across the United States and the world; and WHEREAS, in January 2019, Commissioner Morrison sponsored an ordinance creating the Cook County Committee on Addressing Bias, Equity, and Cultural Competency, which included an incredibly diverse coalition of community leaders, advocates, and community members representing the diversity of Cook County, tasked with the evaluation of policies and procedures relating to addressing bias and creating a more equitable, and culturally competent Cook County Government; and WHEREAS, the hard work of this Committee concluded and culminated in the completion of a Final Report that October, which detailed their work and a roadmap which included recommendations being provided to the Board and President regarding the need for increased training concerning equity, and ultimately lead to the improved training of Cook County’s workforce, better understanding of our diverse communities, and improved equity across Cook County; and WHEREAS, in June of 2019, Commissioner Morrison spearheaded an amendment to the Public Accommodation Section of the Cook County Human Rights Ordinance, which passed unanimously, removing transphobic language from the Cook County Code which provided for the requirement that a government ID be shown to access places of public accommodation; and WHEREAS, this amendment further included clear protections, stating that “each person determines their own gender identity; no proof of an individual’s sex or gender shall be required except for the person’s expression of their own gender”, and amended the definition of sex to include both the biological category as well as gender identity; and WHEREAS, in October of 2019, Commissioner Morrison co-sponsored the VOTE (Voting Opportunity and Translation Equity) Ordinance which expanded the number of languages that suburban Cook County ballots and voting material must be translated into, beyond what was required by the Voting Rights Act; and WHEREAS, as part of the VOTE Ordinance, for every language that has 13,000 or more limited-English-proficient suburban Cook County residents would be guaranteed a fully translated ballot; and WHEREAS, the VOTE Ordinance expanded language access from just four languages to a total of twelve, making Cook County the second most language inclusive County in the nation; and WHEREAS, in 2023, Commissioner Morrison introduced an amendment to the VOTE Ordinance to further protect access to translated voting materials to eligible language populations, ensuring that once the language population in question meets the entry threshold translated materials shall continue to be provided in that language until they reach the minimum threshold of 2,000 Cook County residents; and WHEREAS, along with others in 2020, Cook County Board President Toni Preckwinkle and Commissioner Morrison announced the launch of the Cook County COVID-19 Recovery: Small Business Assistance Program, a business advising, education, and grant program designed to help small businesses through the COVID-19 pandemic, offering an additional $12.6 million in grants to businesses across the county; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison offered his unwavering support for every COVID-19 initiative the County took supporting residents and their needs in the wake of the COVID-19 Pandemic, providing life-saving care and critical funding for our residents and small businesses; and WHEREAS, to ensure District 15 residents had the information they needed during the pandemic, Commissioner Morrison’s Office put out multiple newsletters each month providing every update received on the virus as well providing details about the new initiatives and programs of support put in place and that went online; and WHEREAS, in 2021, Commissioner Morrison co-sponsored the creation of the County’s first Residential Tenant Landlord Ordinance (RTLO), which included protections and affirmed the rights of renters in suburban Cook County who had lacked critical protections against lockouts, excessive move-in and late fees, and exorbitant interest rates on rental arrears; and WHEREAS, in June of 2021, Commissioner Morrison led the charge to have Cook County raise the LGBTQ+ Pride Flag in Daley Plaza for the very first time, a tradition that has become an annual celebration and recognition of the LGBTQ+ community throughout the County; and WHEREAS, also in June of 2021, the Board unanimously passed Commissioner Morrison’s Gender Inclusive Documents and Forms Ordinance, which banned the county from asking a person’s sex assigned at birth on documents and forms unless that information is required for medical reasons, data collection, or by law and that when a person’s sex must be identified, the Ordinance requires the county to ask for a person’s sex assigned at birth as well as gender identity; and WHEREAS, also in June of 2021, Commissioner Kevin Morrison, keeping his pledge of improving transparency throughout Cook County government, passed the Cook County Information Technology Reporting Ordinance, requiring that Separately Elected offices and all Cook County Departments to provide quarterly reports concerning all major technology contracts costing taxpayers $10 million or more dollars before the Technology and Innovation Committee; and WHEREAS, in November of 2021, Commissioner Morrison served as a member of the now retired Small Business Sub-committee, which focused on the needs and of small business owners across the County during the COVID-19 Pandemic; and WHEREAS, as a member of this sub-committee, Commissioner Morrison advocated for the need of a one-stop-shop of small business services and resources, which culminated in 2021 to the creation of the Cook County Small Business Source, a now nationally recognized program, which provides free assistance, advice, and resources to current and aspiring small business owners; and WHEREAS, in 2022, Commissioner Morrison led the creation of Cook County’s first dedicated Department of Mental and Behavioral Health housed within Cook County Health, which offers services promoting mental well-being, early intervention and prevention, as well as the treatment of mental health and substance use conditions; and WHEREAS, the Department of Mental and Behavioral Health describes itself as a convener, funder, and catalyst, bringing together Service Providers, advocates, philanthropic organizations, and community members, to strengthen behavioral health across Cook County, with an ultimate goal of filling every gap in the Mental and Behavioral Health space across the county; and WHEREAS, in May 2022, Commissioner Morrison called for the first hearing on the violence and systemic barriers for transgender residents in Cook County, beginning a discussion regarding current protocols and future plans in serving the transgender community; and WHEREAS, in September 2022, Commissioner Morrison became the first Commissioner to be the Chief Sponsor of an Ordinance eliminating a Cook County Tax, by authoring the Wheel Tax Elimination Ordinance, which permanently repealed the County’s regressive Vehicle Registration Tax, which eliminated the excessive fines and fees associated with it, and created new savings for working families; and WHEREAS, in December of 2022, Commissioner Morrison had the distinct honor of being sworn in to a second term, having earned the trust of his constituents, becoming the first openly LGBTQ+ Cook County Commissioner ever to be reelected to office; and WHEREAS, having spent years advocating and working to expand diversity in leadership, he was proud to support the historic elections of Commissioner Maggie Trevor, Cook County’s first Lesbian board member, and Commissioner Anthony Quezada, Cook County’s first Gay and Latino board member, both elected in 2022, which helped bring to fruition Cook County’s first LGBTQ+ Caucus; and WHEREAS, in 2023, Commissioner Morrison was the Chief Sponsor to an Ordinance amending the Human Rights Ordinance modernizing definitions to remove anti-trans language and include more gender neutral and inclusive language with regards to gender and sexual orientation, as well as include new definitions for bodily autonomy, reproductive health care, assisted reproduction, and gender-affirming care; and WHEREAS, this Ordinance provided for Gender Identity, Reproductive Health choices, and protections against Caste Discrimination be added as Protected Classes for Cook County’s almost 5.2 million residents, making Cook County’s Human Rights Code the most comprehensive and protective Human Rights Codes in America; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison led additional Ordinance amendments to the Human Rights Code later in 2023, modernizing additional language definitions for Indigenous Americans and expanding the definition for unlawful discrimination to include hair texture and protected individual expression as well as an individual’s culture through their hairstyles; and WHEREAS, in April of 2023, the Cook County Board of Commissioners unanimously passed Commissioner Morrison’s Ordinance Amendment calling for a Land Acknowledgment statement to be read at the beginning of regular meetings of the Cook County Board of Commissioners, as well as the Cook County Forest Preserve District meetings, acknowledging that the County exists on lands that have been home to Indigenous People for thousands of years; and WHEREAS, this Land Acknowledgment recognizes the immemorial ties of the Indigenous peoples who resided in Cook County, which includes the Council of Three Fires, the Ojibwa, Ottawa, and Potawatomi, as well as the Miami, Peoria, Ho-Chunk, Menominee, Sauk, and Meskwaki peoples; and WHEREAS, also in 2023 as part of the County’s FY 24 Budget, the Board unanimously approved Commissioner Morrison’s budget amendment spearheading the creation of the Cook County Disaster Response and Recovery Fund, with an initial appropriation of $100 million to be held in a special purpose fund in the case of a declared natural or man-made disasters, to provide emergency funding for communities at their most desperate time of need, a fund that has continued to be replenished every year since its inception; and WHEREAS, in July of 2023, after conversations and work with Commissioner Morrison, the President's Office and the Cook County Bureau of Economic Development announced the launch of the 2023 Source Grant through the Cook County Small Business Source, a program that strove to support, grow and elevate small businesses as the backbone of Cook County’s economy, awarding $40 million in grants to thousands of small businesses across the County in early 2024; and WHEREAS, in 2024, Commissioner Morrison continued the work highlighting the injustices and dangers residents face by co-sponsoring a resolution calling for a hearing to address the human trafficking and sexual exploitation of men and boys in Cook County; and WHEREAS, in 2024, Commissioner Morrison called for an additional hearing on the ongoing violence facing the transgender community to get an update regarding past, current, and future protocols and plans in place which aim at serving and protecting the transgender community; and WHEREAS, as part of his work amplifying the voices of LGBTQ+ representatives across the country, Commissioner Morrison restarted the National Association of County LGBT Leaders and Allies (NACLGBTLA) and served as its Chair from 2023 through July 2026; and WHEREAS, in his role as Chair, Commissioner Morrison led the effort to rename the Affinity Group from NACLGBTA to NAQuO to create a clearer acronym that could be spoken, bringing the affinity group in alignment with other NACo organizations and affinity groups; and WHEREAS, in 2025, in response to the series of unprecedented attacks on immigrant communities and the LGBTQ+ community by the federal government, Commissioner Morrison passed several resolutions dedicated to reaffirming Cook County’s unwavering supporting Due Process and support for youth access to gender-affirming and life-saving care; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison also introduced resolutions condemning the several Senate Democrats that capitulated with Senate Republicans to end the 2025 government shutdown, which failed to reverse the devastating cuts of the Affordable Care Act (ACA) premium tax credits, and highlighting the devastating impact they continue to have on residents across Cook County; and WHEREAS, in May of 2026, Commissioner Morrison called for a hearing regarding the increase in colorectal cancer in young adults within Cook County and the necessary steps to ensure all residents have access to affordable preventative screenings; and WHEREAS, in 2026, Commissioner Morrison also introduced a resolution highlighting the global impacts of Trump Administration’s cuts to USAID and PEPFAR and decrying and calling for the several of these funding cuts, which are projected to lead to 14 million preventable deaths from those experiencing HIV/AIDS, malaria, tuberculosis, and other diseases by 2030, which will come to pass without this critical aid being restored; and WHEREAS, in 2026, following the passage of a contract between the Sheriff’s Office and Insight Public Sector Inc. for Flock automatic license plate readers, Commissioner Morrison championed an Ordinance amendment, in direct alignment with his continued commitment to government transparency, requiring semi-annual reports from the Sheriff to ensure transparency of any documented violations, provide clarity to the repercussions instituted against violators, and to protect residents’ personal data; and WHEREAS, in his time on the Board, Commissioner Morrison served as a leader on improving access to critical health care services such as mental health services, supporting small business development, expanding equity for our diverse communities, and pushing for a fairer and more transparent Cook County government; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison has made a great impact on the County in his time as a member of the Asset, Audit, Business and Economic Development, Contract Compliance, Criminal Justice, Finance, Transportation, Environment and Sustainability, Health and Hospitals, Labor, Law Enforcement, Legislation and Intergovernmental Relations, Pension, and Zoning and Building Committees; and WHEREAS, he has also played a key part in the advancements and changes in the County with his time as Chair of the Technology and Innovation Committee, Emergency Management and Regional Security Committee, the Human Relations Committee, and the Forest Preserve Zoological Committee; and WHEREAS, over the course of his tenure, Commissioner Morrison has worked tirelessly to ensure that all residents are protected and represented no matter their background, identity, or income; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison will vacate his seat on the Cook County Board of Commissioners after eight years of public service to the residents of the 15th district on December 7, 2026; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison, having run for Congress in Illinois’ 8th Congressional District, had to choose between running for reelection and vacating his seat; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison is confident that his legacy will live on through the leadership of his Chief of Staff, Commissioner-Elect Ted Mason, succeeding him as Commissioner of the 15th District; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison will maintain his activism and work advocating for those in need of support, especially as he continues his legal career and practice with Vasselli Law; and WHEREAS, Commissioner Morrison’s life and passion for public service is far from over, and upon his retirement from the Cook County Board at 36 years of age, he will continue seeking any opportunity that avails itself to be of service for the communities he has, and always will, continue to fight for; NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, that Toni Preckwinkle, President, and Members of the Cook County Board of Commissioners do hereby extend their sincere and grateful appreciation to Commissioner Kevin Morrison for his exemplary leadership as a public servant and wish him their best for continued success, happiness, and good health in the years to come; and BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that this text be spread upon the official proceedings of this Honorable Body, and a suitable copy of same be tendered to Commissioner Kevin Morrison.
