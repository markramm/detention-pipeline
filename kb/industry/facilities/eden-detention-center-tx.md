# Eden Detention Center

## Overview

Eden Detention Center is a privately owned and operated men's (and some women's) detention
facility in Eden, Concho County, TX, run by **CoreCivic** (formerly Corrections Corporation of
America) — **not GEO Group**, correcting an operator misattribution in this project's heat-score
signal notes. The facility opened in 1985 under a Federal Bureau of Prisons contract, was sold to
CCA in 1995 for $18M, and was shut down in 2017 when DOJ phased out FBOP's use of private
contractors. It reopened in June 2019 under a new arrangement: an **intergovernmental agreement
between the City of Eden and the U.S. Marshals Service**, with CoreCivic as the operating
contractor and ICE authorized to use capacity at any time. This IGSA-via-city structure (not a
county- or ICE-direct contract) is the same "pass-through municipality" pattern seen at other
CoreCivic/GEO facilities and is worth noting for anyone tracing county vs. city vs. private
contract chains in this KB.

As of the 2019 reactivation, CoreCivic projected $35-40M in annualized revenue from the facility.
The facility is still operating on this contract as of mid-2026, holding both ICE and USMS
populations concurrently — a dual-agency population mix rather than a dedicated ICE processing
center.

## Key Details

- **Capacity**: Facility total ~1,422-1,558 beds (sources vary); 2019 contract split ~660 beds for
  ICE, ~844 for USMS.
- **Population**: Vera Institute ICE Detention Trends data (on file in this KB, `kb/facilities/`)
  shows an average daily ICE population of 641 in recent data pulls; a September 2025 figure cited
  elsewhere put average daily ICE population at ~275 — population appears to fluctuate
  significantly, consistent with ICE's practice of surging/de-surging rural contract beds.
- **Operator**: CoreCivic, under contract with the City of Eden (intergovernmental agreement,
  indefinite term, 30-day termination notice either party) since June 1, 2019. USMS is the prime
  federal counterparty; ICE has standing authority to use capacity.
- **Contract fee structure**: 2023 contract amendment set the City of Eden's administrative fee at
  $1.08 per federal detainee day — the city's cut for serving as the IGSA pass-through.
- **Compliance history**: A DHS Office of the Immigration Detention Ombudsman (OIDO) inspection
  (report dated Nov. 2023) found compliance in 3 of 6 reviewed areas and cited violations for
  insufficient health care staffing, lapsed credential verification for medical staff, and
  water-temperature standards not maintained. A subsequent ICE facility inspection occurred
  Feb. 25-27, 2025 (results not yet located in this search pass — flagged for follow-up).
- **Historical incidents**: Facility has a documented history of unrest predating its ICE/USMS
  era — 1989 food-quality protest/mattress burning, 1989 recreation-scheduling riot (284 inmates),
  1991 quarters refusal (~$250K damage), 1992 recreation-center/library fires, and an August 1995
  SWAT/tear-gas standoff injuring 13+ inmates and staff, with subsequent guard-abuse allegations.
  These predate the 2019 ICE/USMS reactivation (facility was BOP-contracted at the time) but are
  part of the operational record CoreCivic references when marketing the facility's "30-year"
  government-partner track record.
- **Local/community framing**: CoreCivic's own July 2025 facility-spotlight piece emphasizes
  community-relations programming (Easter/Christmas donations to local nursing homes, Fall Fest
  participation, nursing-student tours) — standard CoreCivic messaging for rural host communities
  where the facility is a major local employer in a fiscally distressed county (see
  `kb/budget/concho-county-tx-usda-distress.md`, distress score 4/10).
- **No 2025-2026 closure, expansion, or new-contract news found** in this search pass. No
  Concho-County-specific 287(g) news found; Texas's SB8 (effective Jan. 1, 2026) mandates all
  county sheriffs operating jails cooperate with ICE, which mechanically covers Concho County
  under the same statewide pattern already reflected in the heat-score `287g:8` signal (no
  county-specific action to report beyond that statewide baseline).

## Why It Matters

Eden Detention Center is a long-running (since 1985) rural private-prison/federal-detention hybrid
whose 2019 reactivation via a small-city IGSA pass-through (population ~2,600 town serving as legal
intermediary for a federal contract) is a template worth tracking elsewhere in the buildout — it
lets CoreCivic operate what is functionally an ICE facility without a direct ICE or county
contract, complicating transparency and local-opposition organizing since the City of Eden council,
not Concho County commissioners, is the nominal counterparty.
