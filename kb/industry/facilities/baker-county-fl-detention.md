# Baker County Detention Facility — North Florida "Deportation Depot"

## Overview

The Baker County Detention Facility, located at the former Baker Correctional Institution in Sanderson, Florida, opened **July 1, 2025 / announced operational August 2025**, with capacity to hold over **1,300 detainees**. [[geo-group|GEO Group]] announced a joint-venture agreement in October 2025 to provide management services at what it calls the **North Florida Detention Facility**. As of DeSantis's June 25, 2026 announcement closing "Alligator Alcatraz," Deportation Depot **remains open** and is explicitly the named successor capacity — DeSantis said Alligator Alcatraz helped deport 21,000 people while Deportation Depot, which has processed roughly 10,000-11,000 on its own count, "will remain open."

**Important distinction**: This is a *separate facility* from the long-running **Baker County Detention Center** in Macclenny (the county jail, operating under ICE IGSA #DROIGSA-09-0038 since 2008, run by Sheriff Scotty Rhoden's office) — both are in Baker County and both hold ICE detainees, but they are different buildings with different operators and different histories. The state-run Deportation Depot is at the former Baker Correctional Institution site in Sanderson; the county jail is in Macclenny. National coverage sometimes conflates the two under the "Baker County" name. See the Macclenny facility's own long-running ACLU litigation history below — treat it as a related but distinct signal.

## Key Details

- **Opened**: July 1, 2025 (construction-to-open in days, per reporting); operational status confirmed August/September 2025
- **Capacity**: 1,300+ beds
- **Location**: Former Baker Correctional Institution, Sanderson, FL (~40 miles west of Jacksonville)
- **Management**: [[geo-group|GEO Group]] (joint venture, announced October 2025)
- **Throughput**: 93 deportation flights / ~2,926 deportations reported as of Jan 5, 2026 (DeSantis update); ~10,000 processed cumulatively by June 2026
- **Post-Alligator-Alcatraz role**: Named explicitly by DeSantis as the facility that "remains open" after Alligator Alcatraz's June 25, 2026 closure — i.e., the state's detention-capacity center of gravity shifts entirely to Baker County once the Everglades site closes
- **Funding**: Covered in part by a $608M FEMA grant awarded to Florida (Oct 2025) for both Alligator Alcatraz and Deportation Depot; a federal Environmental and Historic Preservation (EHP) review hold froze disbursement Dec 2025–March 2026 (FEMA denied a $30M request Dec 10, 2025 over incomplete review) until the Trump administration lifted the EHP hold March 13, 2026, allowing Florida to bill up to $249/detainee/night without completing the review. The EHP review remains contested in ongoing NEPA litigation (Friends of the Everglades, Earthjustice, Miccosukee Tribe, Center for Biological Diversity) — see [[bradford-county-douglas-building]] and the "NEPA wins don't travel" pattern; this funding fight is about Alligator Alcatraz/Everglades siting specifically and its resolution doesn't automatically extend to Baker.

## Community Response

Florida residents have organized protests against the facility, with the Independent Florida Alligator covering opposition under the "Deportation Depot" framing (~50 protesters, Jan 2026, organized by Party for Socialism & Liberation, Baker Interfaith Friends, Jacksonville Immigrant Rights Alliance, Florida Immigrant Coalition).

## ACLU Concerns

The ACLU of Florida previously filed a CRCL complaint regarding conditions at the Baker County Detention Center (Macclenny, the county jail — see distinction above), documenting systemic concerns predating the Deportation Depot's 2025 opening. That litigation and complaint history is tracked as a separate facility signal; see the Macclenny IGSA jail's own record.
