# El Valle Detention Facility — Raymondville, TX

## Overview

El Valle Detention Facility is an active ICE detention facility at 1800 Industrial
Drive in Raymondville, Willacy County, TX, operated by Management & Training
Corporation (MTC) under an IGSA (70CDCR23DIG000007). It opened in 2018 **on the
same site as the former Willacy County Correctional Center** — the notorious
Kevlar-tent "Ritmo" (Raymondville's Gitmo) federal facility that a 2015 inmate riot
rendered uninhabitable, forcing its 2015-2017 closure and the transfer of all ~2,800
inmates elsewhere. MTC, the same operator, later rebuilt/reopened the site as El
Valle. This is the currently operational facility on this stretch of Industrial
Drive — the "tent city" itself is closed and gone, but the site did not stay dark.

Average daily population per Vera Institute ICE Detention Trends data: 922 —
making this one of the larger active ICE facilities in South Texas, well above the
county-jail-scale numbers at the nearby Willacy County Regional Detention Center
(see companion entry).

## Key Details

- **Operator**: MTC (Management & Training Corporation), same contractor that ran
  the original Willacy tent-city facility before its 2015 riot closure.
- **IGSA**: 70CDCR23DIG000007 P00001, ICE AOR: Harlingen / San Antonio Field Office.
- **2025 Alien Enemies Act litigation**: Venezuelan men held at El Valle were among
  those the Trump administration attempted to deport in March 2025 under Presidential
  Proclamation 10903 (invoking the Alien Enemies Act against alleged Tren de Aragua
  members). U.S. District Judge Fernando Rodriguez Jr. (a Trump appointee, Southern
  District of Texas) ruled in April/May 2025 that this use of the Act was unlawful,
  blocking the deportations — one of several district rulings nationally that halted
  Alien Enemies Act removals in spring 2025.
- **Bond-hearing exposure**: El Valle detainees sit squarely inside the Fifth
  Circuit, which in *Buenrostro-Mendez v. Bondi* (Feb 6, 2026) upheld indefinite
  no-bond detention under 8 U.S.C. § 1225(b)(2)(A) — see
  [[bond-hearing-circuit-split-2026]]. NOTE FOR FOLLOW-UP: a July 2, 2026 Fifth
  Circuit panel ruling (reported by the Texas Tribune) held the government cannot
  hold undocumented immigrants more than 90 days without a bond hearing — this
  appears to be a *second, later* Fifth Circuit panel decision that may conflict
  with or narrow *Buenrostro-Mendez*. Not yet reconciled with the existing circuit-
  split note as of this write-up; flagging for the next research pass rather than
  asserting a resolution here.

## Why It Matters

El Valle demonstrates that a "closed" notorious facility site does not mean the
county exits the detention pipeline — the same operator (MTC) returned to the same
Raymondville site under a new name and a new IGSA, with a daily population (922)
that dwarfs the original heat-score signal's framing. Willacy County carries both
a live, large-scale ICE facility AND active circuit-split bond litigation risk for
the people held there.
