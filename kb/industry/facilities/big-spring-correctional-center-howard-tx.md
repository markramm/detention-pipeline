# Big Spring Correctional Center (GEO Group)

## Overview

Big Spring Correctional Center (BSCC) is a four-unit, GEO Group-owned prison complex built on the grounds of the decommissioned Webb Air Force Base in Big Spring, Howard County, TX. The four units — **Airpark** (1701 Apron Drive, opened 1991), **Cedar Hill** (3711 Wright Avenue, opened 1998), **Flightline** (2001 Rickabaugh Drive, opened 1995), and **Interstate** (1801 W. I-20, opened 1989, a converted former Ramada Inn) — previously held federal Bureau of Prisons "criminal alien" inmates under contract until BOP's 2021 private-prison wind-down (Executive Order 14006). All four appear on the detention-pipeline heat-score dataset as active IGSA-designated facilities (igsa:10 signal, sourced from Vera Institute), but GEO's own facility pages currently list at least Cedar Hill (924 beds) and Flightline (1,452 beds) as **"Idle" with no active client** — i.e., built, staffed-capable, and sitting in GEO's inventory of idle beds explicitly marketed for ICE reactivation.

GEO Group's Q1 2026 earnings materials describe approximately **6,000 idle high-security beds across six company-owned facilities** — mostly former BOP sites — that GEO says "could be activated within a few months" and would generate roughly $300M/year in combined revenue if filled. Big Spring's two named-idle units (Cedar Hill + Flightline = 2,376 beds) are part of that inventory. As of the Q1 2026 call, management characterized the environment as a "holding pattern" pending policy/appropriations resolution, and Big Spring was **not** among the facilities GEO has already reactivated in 2025-2026 (Delaney Hall NJ, North Lake MI, D. Ray James GA, North Florida Detention Facility, and the reactivated Adelanto CA facility) — meaning Big Spring remains warm, unconverted capacity rather than an already-signed ICE contract.

In August 2022, the Big Spring City Council approved a memorandum of understanding with GEO Group specifically to bring the Flightline unit back online as an ICE detention center, with a city councilman citing the loss of roughly half the site's prior employment after the BOP contracts lapsed. That MOU process appears to be the origin of the "pending" pitch that has not yet converted to a signed ICE contract as of mid-2026.

## Key Details

- **Combined capacity**: Airpark 564 / Cedar Hill 520-924 (sources vary; GEO's own site lists 924) / Flightline 520-1,452 (GEO's own site lists 1,452) / Interstate 417
- **Operator**: GEO Group, Inc. (owns the real estate outright — not leased)
- **Prior contract**: U.S. Bureau of Prisons, "deportable alien" / criminal-alien population, terminated under EO 14006 (2021)
- **Current status**: Idle, no active client (per GEO Group facility pages, checked 2026-07)
- **City posture**: Pro-reactivation — Big Spring City Council approved an MOU with GEO in August 2022 specifically to reopen Flightline as an ICE facility, citing local job losses
- **National context**: Named implicitly among GEO's ~6,000 idle beds / $300M revenue-if-filled inventory disclosed on the Q1 2026 earnings call; GEO CEO George Zoley has also floated selling idle facilities to ICE outright with GEO retained as long-term operator, though no Big Spring-specific sale has been confirmed
- **Adjacent signal**: The same county also carries a 287(g) WSO agreement (Howard County Sheriff's Office, signed October 17, 2025) and Texas SB8's statewide mandatory-287(g) requirement (effective Jan 1, 2026) — meaning the county has both idle bed capacity and an ICE-cooperative sheriff's office simultaneously, a strong convergence pattern.

## Update (November 12, 2026): ICE agreement through the City of Big Spring

- **Council vote (reported Oct 29, 2026):** The Big Spring City Council, in a special session, voted unanimously to approve a 10-year intergovernmental agreement with the federal government to reopen the Flightline and Cedar Hill facilities as immigration detention. Local-news coverage carried by Yahoo News described it as a "$2.5 billion contract" in two phases, the first valued at $170 million, plus $55 million in city-managed funds to rebuild runways and taxiways at McMahon-Wrinkle Airport. The Yahoo News article (byline Lucero Corona, published Oct 29, 2026) states the $2.5 billion, $170 million and $55 million figures and the Nov 1, 2026 start; it does not cite where the numbers come from. GEO's Nov 2 release gives no contract total, only the roughly $200 million first-year revenue estimate. No ICE or city document giving the $2.5 billion total was found (council agenda packet, minutes and Big Spring Herald coverage not located), so treat it as a single-source news figure. GEO pledged up to 1,200 local jobs. GEO representatives said stays would typically be capped at one month, with up to 150 women held in a separate area. Council member Daniel Moreno (District 6) said he would hold GEO to its promises.
- **GEO announcement (announced Nov 2, 2026):** GEO said the City of Big Spring "has entered into a 10-year intergovernmental service agreement with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement" for a federal immigration processing center at the 2,329-bed complex, made up of the 1,404-bed Flightline Facility and the 925-bed Cedar Hill Facility. GEO provides support services including exclusive use of the facility by ICE, security, maintenance, food services, recreation, medical care and transportation. GEO expects approximately $200 million in annual revenue in the first full year of operations. The Yahoo report gives a start date of Nov 1 and an end of October 2036.
- **Structure:** ICE contracts with the city and GEO supplies services, the pass-through IGSA structure. This replaces the "pending" status above. Whether detainees have arrived was not found as of Nov 13, 2026: no report of a first arrival or opening date was located in news searches. Idle-bed figures in the earlier sections (2,961 total, 1,452 and 924 units) differ from the 1,404 and 925 in GEO's release.
- Not opened: the IGSA itself, the council agenda packet and ICE's own announcement.
