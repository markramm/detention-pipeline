# ICE Edinburg Sub Office

## Overview

An unmarked building near Trenton Road and McColl Road in Edinburg, TX has operated as an ICE "Sub Office" used to hold migrants after arrest, despite drawing no public attention until June 2026. Hidalgo County property records show DHS has owned the building since 2021, but the facility's use as an ICE processing/holding site was effectively unknown to the surrounding community until a detained migrant's phone was tracked to the location by his daughter roughly three weeks before the story broke — prompting a small community protest on June 5, 2026.

ICE describes the site as a "processing facility, not a detention facility," where people are quickly processed and then transferred to permanent detention housing elsewhere (e.g., East Hidalgo Detention Center or other South Texas facilities). Community organizers and advocates are skeptical of this characterization, citing the lack of any public signage, notification, or oversight, and reports that people have been held there for extended periods rather than "quickly" transferred.

## Key Details

- **Location**: unmarked building near Trenton Rd & McColl Rd, Edinburg, TX (Hidalgo County)
- **Ownership**: DHS-owned since 2021 — federal government has held the property for roughly five years prior to public awareness of its ICE use
- **Discovery**: A family tracked a detained relative's phone to the building roughly three weeks before June 5, 2026, revealing the site's function to the community
- **Protest**: ~20 protesters gathered June 5, 2026, organized by Chriselda Vera of the grassroots group Blue Bunny Brigade — named for Liam Conejo Ramos, a 5-year-old migrant boy detained while wearing a blue bunny hat
- **ICE response**: Agency confirms the site is a "Sub Office" for processing, disputes "detention facility" framing, says people are quickly transferred to permanent housing at a detention facility
- **Significance**: A second, distinct Hidalgo County ICE site (separate from both the McAllen warehouse purchase and East Hidalgo Detention Center/GEO), and an example of a facility that operated with essentially zero public visibility for years before family-driven discovery forced disclosure

## Why It Matters

Adds a third node to Hidalgo County's detention infrastructure (alongside East Hidalgo Detention Center and the paused McAllen warehouse) and is a clean case study in the "secretive intake site" pattern — federally owned, unmarked, undisclosed, discovered only through informal family tracking rather than any oversight mechanism, and defended post hoc as merely "processing" once exposed.
