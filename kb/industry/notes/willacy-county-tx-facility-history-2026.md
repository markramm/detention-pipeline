# Willacy County, TX — Facility History and Current Status (2025-2026)

## Overview

Willacy County (FIPS 48489) carries a heavy heatmap signal (score 72, igsa:6,
ice-contract:31, anc-contract:16, 287g:8, budget-distress:1) built substantially
on this county's notorious detention history. This note exists to separate what is
**historical/closed** from what is **currently operational**, since the county's
most famous facility — the Kevlar-tent "Ritmo" (Raymondville's Gitmo) federal
prison — closed in 2015-2017 and should NOT be read as an active signal on its own.

## Key Details: Three Distinct Facility Chapters on Two Sites

1. **Willacy County Correctional Center ("Ritmo" / Tent City) — CLOSED, historical
   only.** BOP contract, run by MTC. A February 20-21, 2015 inmate riot (over poor
   medical care and conditions) destroyed the Kevlar tent structures; all ~2,800
   inmates were transferred out; BOP terminated the MTC contract; the site sat dark
   through 2015-2017. **This is the facility the county's national notoriety is
   built on, and it is not operating today.** Any heat-score narrative treating
   "Willacy tent city" as a live threat is stale — the successor facility is what
   matters now.

2. **El Valle Detention Facility — OPERATIONAL, current ICE IGSA.** Built in 2018
   on the *same site* as the tent city (1800 Industrial Drive), same operator
   (MTC), new IGSA contract, current avg. daily population 922. Active in
   2025-2026 litigation over Alien Enemies Act deportations. Full detail:
   [[el-valle-detention-facility-tx]].

3. **Willacy County Regional Detention Center — OPERATIONAL, different site,
   different model.** 1601 Buffalo Drive — Willacy County's own former
   federal/USMS facility, dark after the Biden EO ended MTC's contract in April
   2022, then relaunched via a 50-year lease to neighboring Hidalgo County
   (Oct 2022) with LaSalle Corrections as operator. Functions mainly as Hidalgo
   County's overflow jail, not a direct ICE stream. Full detail:
   [[willacy-county-regional-detention-center-tx]].

## Why It Matters

Willacy is a useful case study in **facility-site persistence**: even after the
most disastrous possible outcome for a detention operator (a riot that destroys the
physical plant and ends the federal contract), the underlying real estate and
county revenue relationship don't disappear — they get reconstituted under a new
name, sometimes with the same operator, sometimes via a different revenue model
(inter-county jail lease instead of direct ICE IGSA). For the pipeline's core
thesis — distressed counties monetizing corrections infrastructure — Willacy shows
**two parallel monetization paths running simultaneously on the same few blocks of
Raymondville**: a direct ICE-facing IGSA (El Valle) and a county-to-county jail
lease (Regional Detention Center) that keeps LaSalle Corrections and USMS/IGSA
infrastructure "warm" for a future federal pivot. Willacy County's own USDA
persistent-poverty budget-distress signal (5/10) is the same underlying condition
that made both deals attractive to county leadership in the first place.
