# Sweetwater County: Commission Ratifies the Sheriff's ICE Agreements After the Fact

## The Fight

Wyoming law, as the ACLU of Wyoming argues in a suit against the Laramie County sheriff, requires county commission approval before a sheriff signs agreements or incurs county expenses. Sweetwater County's sheriff signed 287(g) agreements without a commission vote. In early July 2026 the commission passed a resolution ratifying them retroactively.

## Key Details

- **Ratification (early July 2026):** The ACLU of Wyoming's July 21, 2026 release says "in early July 2026, the Sweetwater County Commission passed a resolution retroactively ratifying all agreements former Sheriff John Grossnickle unilaterally entered into with ICE since 2020." WyoFile reported on Jul 22, 2026 that this came "earlier this month," after the ACLU filed suit against the Laramie County Sheriff's Office, and that Grossnickle retired at the end of May. The commission's resolution, vote count and date were not found; the commission's June and July meeting summaries opened (Citizen Journal, weeks of June 3, June 17, July 8) do not mention it.
- **What was ratified:** Sweetwater and Laramie are the only Wyoming sheriffs' offices holding all three 287(g) types, Jail Enforcement, Warrant Service Officer and Task Force (WyoFile, Aug 31, 2026). See [[sweetwater-county-detention-center-wy]].
- **Sheriff's Office response:** Spokesperson Jason Mower told WyoFile the office "disagrees with the ACLU's legal position" and that a press release's allegations are not judicial determinations.
- **ACLU records requests:** Campbell, Hot Springs, Lincoln and Natrona counties produced no records showing commission consideration before their sheriffs adopted agreements; Carbon County's commission sanctioned its sheriff's decision in 2025; Crook County has a 1998 resolution authorizing its sheriff to make agreements with other agencies. The ACLU also says none of the counties or the Wyoming Highway Patrol had records of rulemaking under the Wyoming Administrative Procedure Act. These are the ACLU's statements.
- **Laramie County suit dismissed (order dated Aug 25, 2026; reported Aug 31; ACLU filed notice of appeal Sept 23, 2026, per its case page):** A Laramie County district judge dismissed the suit for lack of standing on all three claims and wrote that "the underlying question of defendant Kozak's authority and statutory authority remain unresolved" (WyoFile).
- **Sheriff vacancy:** The commission began filling the sheriff vacancy in the week of June 3, 2026 (Citizen Journal).

## Why It Matters

Ratification after the fact answers, for Sweetwater, the commission-approval question the Laramie suit raised, without a court ruling. Whether the new sheriff changed the agreements was not found.
