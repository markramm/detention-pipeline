# Santa Barbara County: Board Bans ICE From County Property, Moves to Block Detention Siting; Jail-Transfer Undercounting Exposed

## The Fight

Santa Barbara County has two linked but distinct fronts in 2025-2026: a preemptive Board of Supervisors campaign to keep ICE off county property and out of unincorporated land, and a jail-level transparency scandal over how many people the Sheriff's Office is actually handing to ICE versus what it reports under state law.

**Board of Supervisors preemptive action (April 21, 2026).** On a three-part measure co-authored by Supervisors Roy Lee and Laura Capps, the Board voted 4-1 (Supervisor Bob Nelson dissenting) to: (a) adopt a "Use and Preservation Policy for County Owned Property and County Parks and Recreation Areas" barring unauthorized use of county property for immigration enforcement; (b) direct election officials to prepare protocols to prevent ICE activity from disrupting June and November 2026 elections, including "Know Your Rights" voting flyers; and (c) direct the Planning and Development Department to analyze land-use, zoning, and permitting tools — including discretionary review and public notice requirements — that could be used to prohibit the siting, expansion, or operation of immigration detention or enforcement facilities in unincorporated Santa Barbara County. All actions were structured as non-CEQA-triggering administrative/policy actions. County Counsel Rachel Van Mullem cautioned supervisors that enforceability "would depend on the specific facts of a situation" when Supervisor Steve Lavagnino pressed for legal clarity, warning against language that gives residents a false sense of security. Sheriff Bill Brown publicly warned of "consequences" from the restrictions.

**Jail-transfer undercounting scandal.** A Santa Barbara News-Press investigation (published April 2, 2026) used federal Deportation Data Project records to find ICE made at least 99 arrests at Santa Barbara County jails in 2025 — while the Sheriff's Office reported only 12 transfers to the California Attorney General under SB 54's mandatory-transfer-reporting regime. The gap is explained by arrests happening in jail lobbies and parking lots rather than through the official "sally port" transfer procedure that SB 54 reporting captures; 27 of the 99 people arrested at county jails in 2025 had no criminal conviction on record, and roughly half of all 2025-area ICE arrests documented showed no prior conviction — a sharp change from 2024. Data through mid-March 2026 showed 26 more jail arrests, again nearly half with no criminal conviction. The story triggered a public reaction from county supervisors and a lompocrecord.com report that "supervisors want notice of 'alarming changes'" in the Sheriff-ICE relationship. At the June 23, 2026 TRUTH Act public forum in Santa Maria, Sheriff Brown told supervisors his office had changed one jail protocol (inviting SCRAM ankle-monitor technicians into a secure area rather than the lobby) and was open to a proposal to notify defense attorneys when ICE inquires about a client in custody — but stated flatly that his office will not count or monitor ICE arrests happening in jail lobbies/parking lots, and will continue honoring ICE transfer requests through official channels.

## Key Details

- **Apr 21, 2026** — Board of Supervisors votes 4-1 on three-part anti-ICE property/siting measure (Lee/Capps authored; Nelson dissents).
- **Apr 2, 2026** — Santa Barbara News-Press investigation publishes: 99 ICE jail arrests in 2025 vs. 12 reported SB 54 transfers; 221 ICE requests to the Sheriff's Office in 2025, 28 notifications, 12 direct transfers, but ~33 more arrested inside jail facilities via federal judicial warrants — a total far exceeding the self-reported SB 54 figure.
- **Apr 5, 2026** — Local officials react publicly to the investigation (News-Press follow-up).
- **Apr 25, 2026** — News-Press: county supervisors split along political lines over a jail-authority bill described as a "referendum on Sheriff Brown."
- **May 12, 2026** — Board sets a hearing (for June 23) on the Annual TRUTH Act Report and Community Forum per Gov. Code §7283.1(d), reviewing ICE's access to individuals in county custody.
- **Jun 23, 2026** — TRUTH Act public forum held in Santa Maria; Sheriff Brown announces limited policy changes but confirms continued ICE cooperation and refusal to track jail-lobby arrests.
- **Jun 24, 2026** — Santa Barbara Independent: "Santa Barbara Sheriff Under Fire over ICE Arrests at County Jails."
- Ongoing community mobilization: ~1,000-person rally (Jan 25, 2026), Jan 30, 2026 nationwide "Day of Action," student walkouts at San Marcos and Santa Barbara high schools (Feb 10, 2026), and an estimated 12,000-person "No Kings" march (Mar 28, 2026) — sustained public pressure that plausibly shaped the Board's April vote.

**Why it matters:** This is a preemptive-defense pattern (Board acting before any facility is sited, similar to other CA counties moving to zone out ICE facilities ahead of federal buildout pressure) combined with a documented transparency-evasion pattern at the jail level — official SB 54 reporting undercounted actual ICE transfers roughly 8-to-1, showing how sanctuary-law reporting mechanisms can be circumvented by shifting arrest locations from the "sally port" (reportable) to jail lobbies/parking lots (unreported). This undercounting pattern is a structural finding relevant to other CA sanctuary counties and should be checked for elsewhere.
