# Rensselaer County: Sheriffs' Federal Suit Against New York's 287(g) Ban

## The Fight

New York's Local Cops, Local Crimes Act, signed May 27, 2026, bars local governments and law enforcement agencies from entering or staying in 287(g) agreements and from holding people under agreements for federal civil immigration violations (Exec. Law 170-k). Agreements in force were declared void and unenforceable on Aug 25, 2026. Rensselaer County, which in 2018 became the first New York county with a 287(g) agreement, did not terminate. On Aug 25, 2026, Sheriff Kyle Bourgault filed suit with 14 other sheriffs, represented by the Federation for American Immigration Reform (FAIR).

## Key Details

- **The suit (filed Aug 25, 2026):** *Bourgault v. Hochul*, 1:26-cv-01637 (N.D.N.Y.), against Gov. Kathy Hochul, Attorney General Letitia James and the State of New York. Claims: Contracts Clause, Tenth Amendment, Supremacy Clause and the state constitution (home rule and separation of powers). The Aug 26 motion for a preliminary injunction covered Exec. Law 170-k and 63-e, the latter creating the Office of Immigrant Trust.
- **Rensselaer facts (from the sheriff's declarations, as summarized by the court):** the current 287(g) agreement was signed in March 2020, on the Jail Enforcement Model; the jail has 432 beds; the sheriff signed a U.S. Marshals Service agreement on Nov 1, 2024 at $136 per day and a June 27, 2024 agreement with CBP at $130 per night. He said his officers do not make community arrests or traffic stops for immigration status. He received the Attorney General's July 24, 2026 letter, did not answer by Aug 14, and as of Aug 24 had not terminated the agreement or refused a detainer. The Office of Immigrant Trust sent an Aug 19 demand for documents.
- **Ruling (Nov 4, 2026, ordered):** Judge D'Agostino denied the injunction in a 28-page decision. She held the sheriffs had not established a likelihood that they have capacity to sue the state for the alleged constitutional violations, since New York law generally bars local governments and officials from challenging state statutes unless an exception applies, and the sheriffs argued only the "dilemma" exception. She did not reach the merits. The same court had earlier denied a separate Justice Department request (*United States v. New York*, 1:26-cv-1360) to block the same ban. The defendants' answer is due Dec 3, 2026.
- **State enforcement suit (filed Aug 27, 2026):** The Attorney General filed a hybrid Article 78 and declaratory proceeding in Rensselaer County Supreme Court against the sheriff, the Sheriff's Office and the County, seeking an order compelling termination and a declaration that the agreement is void. Hochul and James announced it the same day (Troy Record). No ruling was found.
- **Sheriff's response (Nov 5, 2026):** Bourgault told CBS6, "We are reviewing the ruling and determining the next course of action," and said the office uses screening of people booked into the jail. County Executive Steve McLaughlin said Aug 25 the county would fight to keep the agreement.
- **Related:** The ban also covers jail-bed IGSAs; per the court summary, Rensselaer's jail agreements are with the Marshals and CBP. See [[ny-nassau-287g-deputization-lawsuit]] and [[camden-oneida-county-ny-287g]].

## Why It Matters

The ruling leaves the ban in force while the case continues and decides nothing on whether it is constitutional.
