# Brevard County FL: Sheriff Ivey's 287(g) Buildout, "Operation One Way Ticket," and Student Walkouts

## Why It Matters

Brevard County (Space Coast) carries a heat score of 94 -- driven by heavy ice-contract (16), 287(g) (6), igsa (4), and anc-contract (2, CSI Aviation's $258.5M ICE Brevard obligation) signal density -- yet had no dedicated county-fight file despite being one of the most enforcement-active counties in Central Florida. Brevard illustrates the "compliant sheriff, aggressive posture" pattern: unlike Broward's reluctant Sheriff Tony, Brevard's Sheriff Wayne Ivey has been an enthusiastic, publicly visible partner to ICE and DeSantis -- while still colliding with the federal government (deputy poaching) and the state (reimbursement shortfall), and now facing organized youth resistance.

## The Buildout: "Operation Shield" and Dual 287(g) Models

BSO runs a self-branded immigration enforcement initiative, "Operation Shield," layered on top of Florida's statewide 287(g) mandate (all 67 sheriffs signed on by Feb. 26, 2025, per Gov. DeSantis). Brevard uses **both** 287(g) models:
- **Warrant Service Officer (WSO)** -- operated primarily through the Brevard County Jail Complex, serving immigration detainers on people already booked.
- **Task Force Model (TFM)** -- extends into the field via municipal partners: Satellite Beach PD, Rockledge PD, and West Melbourne PD, plus the Sheriff's Office itself (the heatmap's "multiple TFM + WSO" signal).

Thirty-five BSO members went through 287(g) training as the program stood up in early 2025. Ivey has appeared alongside DeSantis and Lt. Gov. Jay Collins to publicize the enforcement posture.

## "Operation One Way Ticket" (September 22-26, 2025)

A four-day joint operation across Cocoa, Melbourne, Palm Bay, and Viera -- run by BSO with ICE, U.S. Border Patrol, and Florida Highway Patrol -- focused on traffic enforcement, known-location checks, and worksite enforcement. Results: **354 people identified for deportation**, with **150 removed within the operation window**, per Sheriff Ivey. It was folded into a larger ICE Miami-led sweep that hit **more than 400 arrests** (and per ICE's own release, 1,120 criminal-alien arrests) across Central Florida the same week, described by ICE as the largest joint immigration operation in Florida history. BCSO also confirmed deputies assisted a separate ICE worksite action in Cocoa in June 2025 that swept up a construction crew at an apartment complex.

## Friction With ICE: Deputy "Poaching"

In late 2025/early 2026, ICE's deputy director emailed a direct recruitment pitch -- a $50,000 bonus over five years -- to more than 80 BSO deputies, using contact information Ivey believes ICE obtained through the 287(g) partnership itself. Ivey publicly called it a betrayal that "undermines the trust between local law enforcement and federal partners." Brevard is not alone: multiple Florida sheriffs raised the same complaint, but Ivey was a named, quoted voice in the pushback -- notable because he has otherwise been one of DeSantis's most cooperative sheriffs on immigration.

## Funding: A Reimbursement Discrepancy

Florida CFO Blaise Ingoglia visited the Space Coast in February 2026 to present state 287(g) reimbursement checks: **$577,021.44 to BSO**, plus $99,515 to Cocoa Beach PD and $15,102 to Indialantic PD, part of a statewide fund reimbursing agencies for immigration-enforcement costs (overtime, etc.). As of March 2026, however, the state's own transparency website showed only **$21,500** actually disbursed to BSO against that promised amount -- a gap BSO's public information office did not explain when asked by reporters. This mirrors a broader pattern NOTUS documented of Florida sheriffs statewide waiting on promised 287(g) reimbursements.

## Resistance: Student Walkouts (February 2026)

Brevard Public Schools warned students in early February 2026 that walkouts to protest ICE would result in discipline, up to expulsion per School Board Chair Matt Susin. Despite the threat, dozens of students at three high schools -- **Viera, Rockledge, and Satellite** -- walked out on **February 6, 2026**, chanting and holding signs protesting ICE raids and invoking the killings of Alex Pretti and Renée Good (federal agents' fatal shootings during Minneapolis's Operation Metro Surge, not local Brevard incidents, but cited by Brevard students as the national stakes of the enforcement buildout). Fewer than 50 students were confirmed to have left campus without authorization; the district said those students would face detention or suspension. A second round of protests followed on a school day off, staged outside the Brevard Public Schools administrative building, with a further protest calling out the school board on February 16, 2026. This is the first documented organized youth-resistance activity tied to the detention pipeline in Brevard County.

## Key Actors

- **Sheriff Wayne Ivey (BSO)** -- architect of "Operation Shield," public partner to DeSantis/ICE, but pushed back hard on ICE's deputy recruitment
- **Gov. Ron DeSantis / Lt. Gov. Jay Collins** -- state political sponsors of the enforcement posture, appeared at Brevard press events
- **FL CFO Blaise Ingoglia** -- distributed (on paper) state 287(g) reimbursement funds to Brevard agencies
- **School Board Chair Matt Susin** -- threatened expulsion for student walkout participants
- **ICE Miami / U.S. Border Patrol / FHP** -- operational partners in "Operation One Way Ticket"
