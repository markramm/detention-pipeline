# Garza County TX — State Preemption Defeats a Private-Detention Bid at Dalby

## The Fight

Garza County (Post, TX; pop. ~6,000) owns the Giles W. Dalby Correctional Institution, a 251,000-sq-ft, ~1,900-bed private facility built by the county in 1997-98. MTC (Management and Training Corporation) operated it for the federal Bureau of Prisons, historically holding "deportable alien" federal inmates who transitioned to ICE custody after sentence completion — but MTC ended its contract in **September 2024**, closing the facility and costing roughly 200 local jobs.

With the facility vacant, the county ran a competitive operator solicitation in early 2025. **CoreCivic, LaSalle Corrections, and MTC** all submitted proposals. On a county commissioners vote in **March 2025**, Judge Lee Norman announced **CoreCivic as the winning bidder with 91% of evaluation points** — CoreCivic's offer: $1M down plus $27/day per diem for the first 1,000 inmates, with ancillary fees to the county. This was a classic county-owned-facility-to-private-operator handoff of exactly the kind this KB tracks (Sabot-style: distressed rural county, vacant county-owned carceral asset, private operator pitch).

**The state of Texas outbid the county's own process.** Texas House Bill 500 (89th legislative session, 2025) set aside **$110 million** to purchase the Dalby facility outright for the Texas Department of Criminal Justice (TDCJ). Garza County commissioners voted to create a foundation to manage the $100M+ balance of the purchase proceeds rather than proceed with the CoreCivic contract. The state completed its purchase in 2025, TDCJ began repopulating the unit with state inmates in January 2026, and held a ribbon-cutting/reopening ceremony on **February 24, 2026**.

## Key Details

- **Sept 2024**: MTC ends operating contract; facility closes; ~200 jobs lost
- **Feb 13, 2025**: County commissioners begin reviewing operator proposals
- **March 2025**: CoreCivic selected as top-scoring bidder (91% of points) over LaSalle and MTC
- **March 24-25, 2025**: State of Texas surfaces competing purchase offer (HB 500, $110M)
- **2025**: County accepts the state's offer instead of the CoreCivic contract; creates a foundation to hold ~$100M of proceeds
- **Jan 2026**: TDCJ begins moving state inmates into the unit
- **Feb 24, 2026**: Official TDCJ reopening ceremony as the "Giles W. Dalby Unit" — ~900 inmates housed at ceremony time, capacity ~1,900
- **Current status (as of research)**: Facility is a **Texas state prison (TDCJ)**, not an ICE or private-federal-detention facility. Houses a Recovery Housing Opportunity / Substance Use Education Program unit alongside general population. Local officials (Judge Norman, Commissioner Giles W. Dalby Jr.) publicly praised the state for rehiring local staff.

## Why This Matters

This is a variant of the "buildout owner-withdrawal pattern" this KB tracks, but inverted: rather than an owner refusing to sell defeating a warehouse-detention pitch, here a **state government's acquisition preempted a private-detention operator (CoreCivic) that had already won the county's own bidding process**. The county was fully willing to go with CoreCivic — this was not community resistance or a blocked deal, it was the state simply outbidding the private operator for its own (non-ICE) correctional purposes. The practical effect for detention-pipeline tracking is the same as an owner-withdrawal: **the ICE/private-detention pathway at this site is closed**, at least for now, because the facility is now committed to state DOC use. Any future ICE interest would require a new deal with TDCJ, a materially higher bar than the original county-to-private-operator handoff this KB is built to catch early.

Note also that Texas SB 8 (2025) made 287(g) agreements **mandatory** for nearly all Texas counties — so Garza County's WSO agreement (signed July 7, 2025, per Prison Policy Initiative) is very likely compliance with state mandate rather than a discretionary "cooperative sheriff" signal of the kind this KB weights as meaningful in other states.
