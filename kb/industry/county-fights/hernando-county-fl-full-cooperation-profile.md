# Hernando County FL: Full-Stack 287(g) Cooperation, State-Funded, No Local Resistance

## The Fight

There isn't one. Hernando County is a clean example of the opposite pattern from most `county-fights` entries: a jurisdiction with no institutional resistance to ICE cooperation at any level (sheriff, commission, or organized public opposition found in search). Sheriff Al Nienhuis has held office since 2011 (reelected 2024) and has run **all three 287(g) models simultaneously** — Warrant Service Officer (WSO, signed May 6, 2019 — one of only five FL counties in the program that early), Jail Enforcement Model (JEM, signed June 9, 2020), and Task Force Model (TFM, signed Feb 26, 2025, added under the statewide mandate). This "full-stack" combination is itself a notable signal — most counties run one or two models, not three.

Nienhuis has been explicit and public that the county is "not a sanctuary county" and will hold any individual for whom ICE provides "probable cause supported by an affidavit or a warrant from a judge" — a formulation that tracks the post-*Ohio v. ICE*-era constitutional floor for detainer compliance rather than a broader "best efforts" standard some other FL counties (e.g., Orange County) are litigating around.

## Key Details

**287(g) full-stack timeline:**
- WSO: signed May 6, 2019 (early adopter — one of 5 FL counties at the time)
- JEM: signed June 9, 2020
- TFM: signed Feb 26, 2025 (post-mandate; FL law required all counties to have a 287(g) agreement by April 2025 per SB signed Feb 2025)

**Operation Tidal Wave (April 2025–ongoing):** Hernando County Sheriff's Office was named by the Governor's office as one of thirteen FL sheriffs' offices providing "significant assistance" to ICE during Operation Tidal Wave, the largest joint federal-state immigration enforcement operation in Florida history (10,400+ arrests statewide since April 2025; 1,120 arrests in a single weeklong surge reported by ICE).

**State funding:** On January 15, 2026, Florida CFO Blaise Ingoglia announced $1.78 million in reimbursements to five FL law enforcement agencies (Polk, Hernando, Sarasota, Hardee Sheriff's Offices + Port Richey PD) for 287(g)-related costs, including officer training bonuses. Hernando's share: **$321,112.30**. Funding flows through the Florida State Board of Immigration Enforcement. Nienhuis quote: "The ability to aid ICE with immigration efforts means that we can now take a proactive approach to removing criminal illegal aliens from our communities before they have the opportunity to inflict harm on our residents."

**Commission-activity signal clarification (important — resolves heatmap ambiguity):** The two `commission-activity` entries on file for Hernando (BOCC votes Oct 28, 2025 and Apr 14, 2026) are construction/maintenance contracts for the *existing* Hernando County jail — "Detention Center Bravo Roof Replacement" ($312,258.87 to Allied Roofing) and "Detention Center Bravo Rooftop Air Conditioning Unit Replacement" ($1,214,000.00 to Air Mechanical and Service Corporation). These are facilities-maintenance line items on a county jail building named "Bravo," **not** new ICE detention facility construction or a warehouse-conversion project. No evidence found of any new-build, warehouse-purchase, or capacity-expansion proposal for Hernando County as of July 2026 — this county is not part of the ICE Detention Reengineering Initiative buildout list found in current reporting (that reporting names Orlando/Orange County, $100M, among FL sites).

**IGSA / jail population:** Vera Institute data lists the Hernando County Jail as an active IGSA facility with average daily ICE population of only 2 — a small-scale, low-volume hold arrangement consistent with a booking/transfer county rather than a regional detention hub.

## Why It Matters

Hernando is a useful negative-space data point for the Sabot-style targeting pattern: full ideological/operational alignment (three-model 287(g) stack, state funding, Tidal Wave participation, sheriff public messaging) with **no corresponding capacity buildout** — no new facility, no warehouse conversion, no contractor construction contract. It shows that "high cooperation" and "detention infrastructure expansion" are separable signals; Hernando is a cooperation-maximalist county that ICE/DHS have not (yet) selected as a capacity site, unlike Bradford or Orange County FL where commission votes on actual new bed capacity are underway.
